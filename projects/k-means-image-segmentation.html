K-Means Clustering & Image Segmentation

Implemented batch and online K-means in Julia, investigated convergence and sensitivity, and reduced RGB images to small representative color palettes.

JuliaK-meansMonte Carlo

Project overview

This graduate machine-learning project explored how unsupervised learning behaves under different sample sizes, initializations, learning rates and image inputs. The algorithms were implemented from first principles rather than used as black-box library functions.

Methods & workflow

  1. Generated synthetic multivariate Gaussian datasets and calculated Euclidean nearest-centroid assignments.
  2. Implemented batch assignment/update steps with convergence checks and online centroid updates.
  3. Repeated experiments across 100 and 500 trials to evaluate centroid estimation and the number of iterations to converge.
  4. Evaluated 31 online-learning rates with 30 trials per rate.
  5. Applied K-means color quantization to RGB images with 4, 8 and 16 colors, including cross-image palette comparisons.

Results & takeaways

The reported batch experiments converged in 2–7 iterations. More observations generally improved centroid estimation in the experiments, and different starting centroids could produce different solutions. Color palettes trained on one image did not necessarily transfer well to an unrelated image.

Scope and interpretation. These are experiment-specific observations, not universal guarantees of convergence speed or segmentation quality.
