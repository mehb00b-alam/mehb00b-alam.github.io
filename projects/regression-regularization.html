Regression, Model Selection & Regularization

Built regression methods from scratch and explored overfitting, train/test errors, polynomial degree, L2 regularization and sample size.

JuliaLeast squaresPseudoinverse

Project overview

This project examined how least-squares models generalize beyond their training samples, using synthetic problems where model complexity and noise could be controlled.

Methods & workflow

  1. Implemented bivariate, multivariate and polynomial least-squares regression using matrix methods and pseudoinverse calculations.
  2. Simulated noisy linear and nonlinear datasets; recovered known coefficients to within about 0.15 in a reported experiment.
  3. Compared training and test RMS error for polynomial degrees 0 through 9 on a separate 500-sample simulated test set.
  4. Studied ridge/L2 regularization and how coefficient shrinkage depends on penalty strength.
  5. Compared models trained on 15 and 100 samples to understand data availability and overfitting.

Results & takeaways

In the reported setting, polynomial degrees 3–8 gave test RMS error around 0.36–0.38, while a degree-9 model reached almost zero training error but approximately 1.35 test RMS error. L2 regularization reduced extreme coefficient magnitudes; excessively strong regularization could underfit.

Scope and interpretation. Metrics describe simulated class-project datasets and should not be treated as results on a real-world benchmark.
